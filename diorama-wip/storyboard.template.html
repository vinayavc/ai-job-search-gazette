Tiny Diorama · storyboard · 17 s

The Sausage Gazette: from fetch to apply

Six beats on one floating tile. The mail van links the Gazette press, your house and the hiring office. The app's header plays each beat when its stage happens; beats 5 and 6 play only after you say you applied on the job site.

“The mailman brings it to your door”

Moves
The van drives the S-road and pulls up at the bend past your house, leaving the porch clear. The mailman pops out, hops to the porch and drops the paper by the sleeping dachshund, who wakes with ears up and a wag, then hops back into the van. The house sign flips red.

35.25 – 7.6 sRANK

“Scored for you. You circle your pick.”

Moves
The dog sits and the Gazette unrolls on the step. Three ad cards pop up over the porch, and a red rubber stamp thumps a fit score onto each (61, 88, 54). Then a marker ring draws itself around the 88: that is your circle, made in the newspaper below. The other two drop away and the circled ad flies into the typewriter.
Camera
Close on the porch, rising a little so the cards sit in the upper third.
Sound
Three rising pops, three rubber-stamp thumps, a marker stroke, two swishes, a whoosh into the typewriter.

47.6 – 11.2 sTAILOR

“Your pup types up your CV and letter”

Moves
The dog sits at the little typewriter at the end of the step and types. A page marked CV pops up, then one marked COVER LETTER. They fold into a red envelope; the dog carries it to the letterbox, rears up and posts it, and the flag pops up. Then it trots back to the mat and curls up. Nothing has been sent: the flag stays up, waiting for you.
Camera
Holds on the porch, drifting from the typewriter to the letterbox.
Sound
Typing, two typebar clacks with paper, the carriage bell, a paper fold, paw tocks, letterbox clunk and flag click.

511.2 – 13.6 sYOU APPLY

“You apply on the job site. Off it goes.”

Moves
Played only after you tell the app you applied. The front door swings open and you (a peg person) step out, hop round to the letterbox and take the letter; the flag drops. The mailman hops over, you hand it across, wave, and go back in as the door closes. The dog lifts an ear, then sleeps on.
Camera
Stays on the porch, then starts to follow the mailman back to the van.
Sound
Door click, hop tocks, letterbox click and paper, a little handover blip, waves, satchel pop, the door thud.

613.6 – 17.0 sSENT

“Sent. Time for a nap.”

Moves
Dusk falls as the van drives to the office: the sun sinks orange, the sky cools, and the street lamp and your windows catch. The mailman slides the letter through the office mail slot; the office lights up window by window, a clerk pops out and hops, and the office sign flips red. The last second holds on the lit office, with the dog asleep at home.
Camera
Travels with the van to the office and ends in a three-quarter view; the lens stops down a little for the final picture.
Sound
Engine, lamp click, brake swish, hop tocks, envelope slide and slot tock, five rising window blips, sign ding (G) and a soft success chime.

Assumptions

  • Stage mapping in the header: beats 1–2 play while /scrape runs, beat 3 while /rank runs and beat 4 while /apply runs. Beats 5–6 play only after you answer “Posted it?” with yes. With nothing running, the header shows the living scene: the dog asleep on the mat, the time of day following your clock.
  • The letterbox flag stays up whenever you have tailored applications you haven't sent yet: a quiet reminder, not a badge.
  • You circle ads in the newspaper below the header; the ring in beat 3 stands for that click. The dachshund never circles for you.
  • The ad cards show generic role words (Frontend, Backend, Data) and made-up fit scores (61, 88, 54), not your real jobs. Fit scores in the repo run 0–100.
  • “You” are a peg person in mustard, like the other peg people; the mailman wears slate blue with a red satchel. The van, the newspaper band, the score stamps, your circle and the envelope share the one accent red.
  • The radio lives in the page, not in the diorama.
  • Length is 17 s, longer than 12, because six beats are needed to keep “documents ready” and “you applied” separate.
  • In the header the same scene renders into a wide, short strip with its own camera framing; this storyboard shows the 16:9 explainer.
